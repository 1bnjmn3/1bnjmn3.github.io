Leakage-aware surface-code decoder

Independent research · heralded erasure on a rotated surface code

  • A simulation study of what happens if a surface-code decoder is told where a qubit leaked.
  • Leakage is modelled as heralded erasure (Stim's HERALDED_ERASE): a simplified stand-in for leakage, not leakage itself. Real leakage persists, spreads, and is heralded late. None of that is modelled here.
  • Two decoders see the same shots: static PyMatching, which ignores the herald flags, and adaptive PyMatching, which uses them shot by shot.

Qubit-saving ratios are extrapolated from three-point fits (d = 3, 5 and 7), out to distances far beyond those simulated. They illustrate what the fitted suppression factors imply, and are not a measurement. At p_L = 2×10⁻² the 10⁻⁶ window-target ratio is 4.78× (4.74× when distances are rounded to the next odd integer). At p_L = 2×10⁻³, equal to the Pauli noise, that ratio is 1.23× (1.00× on odd-integer patches). The published range across the window-target column is 1.11×–4.78×.

A C++ union-find decoder with erasure support sits in the same repo and is timed against a 1 µs-per-round budget. Those times are software times on a cloud CPU, not hardware latencies. The decoder is unweighted, decodes a whole block rather than streaming, and has not been written in RTL or run on an FPGA. On a CPU it does not meet a hard 1 µs-per-round budget: the worst shot is already several microseconds per round from d = 5 up. Treat the C++ timings as order-of-magnitude and optimistic relative to a real-time hardware decoder.

This is a student project, built with AI assistance; I designed the experiments and can explain every result.
